WHAT CHANGED
  1. Z press offset (3 mm). Was absent entirely. The pen went exactly to the
     drawing plane, so any error in that plane showed as a missing line rather
     than a light one - which is the whole reason the holder is spring loaded.

     lift 20 -> 30 mm.

     up on BASE[2] for a large sheet silently reverted to the small worktable
     base after the first pen change. The paper base and the magazine base are
     now separate variables, and every $BASE write goes through PEN_USE_BASE().
  4. Curved-surface drawing. drawGeo supplied the tool NORMAL but not the tool
     POSITION, so a flat drawing over a scanned sheet kept its flat coordinates
     and the pen would have drawn in mid-air. Strokes are now projected onto
     drawGeo.

NOTES ON TWO OF THESE

"press moves the tip only along the pen axis" looks like trivia and is not.
A press that leaked into the in-plane geometry would distort every drawing by
an amount that scales with the press - invisible on screen, obvious on paper.
Measured sideways shift: exactly zero.

"vertical paper: press is 3 mm into the wall, not 3 mm down" is the orientation
requirement applied to the new number. Press follows the pen axis, so on a wall
it goes into the wall. If it had been written as a world-Z offset this check
would read 3 mm out and the drawing would be a smear.

The curved-sheet test pulls points up to 170 mm onto a large cylinder, lands
them on it to 1.4e-14 mm, and the orientation self-test still passes with the
projection running - closest point commutes with rotation, so the guarantee
survives.

The "usable" in "usable planes come out" is what the middle block checks, and
it is worth being explicit about what usable means for KUKA|prc:

  - every plane is VALID and ORTHONORMAL, because prc reads A/B/C off the
    frame and a skewed frame silently produces a wrong orientation rather
    than an error;
  - every origin lies ON the mesh, to 1.3e-6 mm;
  - every tool axis points INTO the material, not out of it - checked by
    probing 2 mm along +Z and asking the mesh whether that point is inside;
  - the tree structure matches the slices, so Tree Branch selects one cut.

TWO THINGS THAT LOOK LIKE FAILURES AND ARE NOT

The first attempt at this test used a rectangular cross-section and the
self-test came back FAIL with "worst rotation drift 180.000000000 deg". That
was the test model, not the code: a rectangle is unchanged by a half turn
about the long axis, so the part is genuinely two shapes at once and no
arithmetic can prefer one. FL-01 detected it, refused to pick, and named the
reason - which is the behaviour the README promises. The re-run above keeps
that case deliberately, as a check that the ambiguity stays REPORTED.

The first attempt also asserted targets lie on the mesh to 1e-6 mm and
measured 2e-6. Rhino stores mesh vertices as single-precision floats, so that
tolerance is below what the format can represent. The assertion was wrong, not
the code; it is 1e-4 above.
